# Deduplication and Canonical Records

## Search Before Creating

Search the destination and related authoritative systems using:

- canonical subject name and common aliases;
- acronyms, product names, model numbers, people, clients, or project identifiers;
- source URLs and external IDs;
- likely titles and headings;
- practical questions the record should answer;
- distinctive phrases when allowed.

Fetch or open plausible matches in full. A search hit proves similarity, not duplication.

## Compare the Durable Job

Compare each candidate by:

- owning subject or initiative;
- purpose and questions answered;
- audience and access level;
- evidence and authority;
- version, geography, time period, and lifecycle;
- destination and source-of-truth status;
- privacy and retention requirements.

Records are duplicates only when these dimensions substantially match. Similar topics may need separate records when their owners, audiences, evidence standards, or lifecycles differ.

## Choose One Action

- `reused`: the current canonical record already meets the need.
- `updated`: new evidence improves the same canonical record.
- `merged`: multiple fragments have the same durable job and can safely become one record.
- `linked`: records are related but legitimately distinct.
- `created`: no suitable canonical record exists and the proposed record has a distinct durable job.
- `not stored`: the material is transient, unsupported, sensitive, redundant, or not useful enough for durable capture.

## Merge Safely

- Confirm the canonical owner and destination before merging.
- Preserve unique evidence, history required for current decisions, and source links.
- Do not overwrite original source material when preservation matters; create a synthesis instead.
- Require approval before deleting, archiving, redirecting, or moving material when those actions are destructive or change authority.
- Leave a clear pointer from a retained legacy location when the destination supports it and the migration plan requires one.
- Verify that searches return the canonical record and do not prioritize stale duplicates.

## Prevent Future Duplication

- Maintain a landing page or manifest with canonical topics and aliases.
- Give each topic one clear canonical home.
- Put changing status in operational tracking, not copied into evergreen pages.
- Prefer links and compact pointers over copied sections.
- Record the source, owner, freshness, and review trigger on canonical records.
